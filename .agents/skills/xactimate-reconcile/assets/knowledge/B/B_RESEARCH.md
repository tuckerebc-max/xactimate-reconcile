## 2. The 2026 amendments are enacted and in effect, subject to event-specific application

SB 1206 became Laws 2026, Chapter 157. The enacted law says the governor approved it June 19, 2026 and it was filed June 22. It amends §§ 20-321, 20-321.02 and 32-1158.02. The seven-page law contains no special effective-date, emergency or retroactivity clause. The Legislature lists **September 12, 2026** as the 2026 regular session’s general effective date. Reading those records together supports September 12 as the amendments’ effective date. This is a legal-source inference from the enacted law and official date table, not a date printed in each codified section. [B-S05, B-S06]

The changes include an express definition of adjusting, added conduct within the compensated-adjuster definition, restrictions on adjuster solicitation during an active loss/emergency, direct or indirect participation in repairs on an adjusted claim, payment endorsements, deductible inducements, express contractor/adjuster role separation, and contractor solicitation during an active loss/emergency. The permission-plus-uncompensated-communication language in § 32-1158.02(L) was retained; it was not a new general contractor exemption. The session-law amendment markings distinguish new language from existing protections. [B-S01, B-S03–B-S05]

**Record multiple dates.** The loss date selects the relevant policy period but does not alone answer which law governs later solicitation, contracting, adjusting or repair conduct. Collect policy issuance/renewal and endorsement dates, loss date, contract date, each disputed communication, adjusting date, and repair date. Section 1-244 states the default against retroactive statutes. No express retroactivity provision was found here. Application to conduct after September 12 on a pre-September-12 loss is a review question, not a reason to automatically apply either old or new law to everything. [B-S07]

**Scope of the post-storm section matters.** Section 32-1158.02(A) expressly describes direct damage from a catastrophic storm in a specific insurer-designated area, involving a licensed contractor and a purchaser who lives or intends to live in the residence. Obtain the designation and qualifying facts before asserting protections expressly tied to A. Other subsections, including L and O, use their own operative language; counsel should resolve any boundary question. Failure to prove A does not confer permission to adjust without a license or ignore other restrictions. [B-S04]

For qualifying contracts, preserve the separate cancellation routes: **72 hours after insurer denial notification** and **four business days after signing**. Also check the ten-day return-of-payments provision and itemized emergency-services exception; the 50% down-payment cap; signed change orders; inspection/damage disclosures; and the restriction on beginning work before approval or denial, subject to preventing further loss. These are contractor/owner rules, not insurer response deadlines. Do not convert hours into business days. [B-S04(A)–(F), (I)–(J)]

## 4. Policy, causation and competing interpretations

Section 20-1119(A) requires construction of the entire insurance contract, including attached amendments. Obtain declarations, forms/edition dates, all endorsements, renewal notices, exclusions, loss-settlement provisions, duties after loss, appraisal, loss-payment and suit-limitation provisions. A declarations page or agent’s summary is insufficient. A roof payment schedule, ACV-only endorsement, cosmetic limitation, wind/hail deductible or matching exclusion can change the result. [B-S16]

Keggi states the general allocation that the insured establishes coverage under the insuring clause and the insurer establishes an exclusion’s applicability. It is a commercial-liability case, so this is a starting framework; conditions, exceptions to exclusions and particular litigation posture need their own analysis. Do not convert the insurer’s exclusion burden into an obligation to pay every unsupported contractor line. [B-S17 ¶ 13]

For each observed defect, separate: supplied observation; claimed storm connection; alternative explanation; accepted carrier finding; inference; and unanswered question. Consider pre-existing damage, wear, maintenance, faulty installation, foot traffic and later events. Weather records can support timing and event plausibility but do not prove damage to a particular roof. If an exclusion, concurrent-causation clause or resulting-loss exception is material, map the precise wording to the facts and route its interpretation for legal review. These are recommended evidence methods; no actual weather or roof findings were obtained.

Present fair alternatives. A carrier may accept some storm damage while reasonably disputing the area, method, causation or cost of additional work. The response should answer that specific objection, and it should concede an unsupported or duplicate item. A larger estimate and use of Xactimate are not proof of entitlement. This run did not establish that any price database is legally conclusive.

## 5. Replacement, repair, materials and local code

**No controlling universal matching/full-replacement entitlement was established in this bounded search.** That is a research limitation, not proof that no fact-specific right can exist. The materials reviewed include the Arizona statutes/rule, Walker, Hanson and the roofing orders in Hanks, Trudel and Chew. Do not import another state’s matching rule, an automobile provision, or a general market custom into an Arizona roof claim.

Hanks concerned a matching exclusion and notice/expectations arguments; the insured’s partial-summary-judgment motion was denied because factual issues remained. Trudel likewise left endorsement/notice and reasonable-expectations issues for factual resolution when denying the insurer’s motion. Neither order establishes a statewide rule that discontinued tile or an HOA demand automatically requires insurer-funded replacement. These are federal trial orders, persuasive and fact-specific, not controlling Arizona appellate matching holdings. [B-S14, B-S15]

Chew illustrates the weakness of a replacement estimate that does not explain whether its scope addresses storm damage or other causes. It also shows why appraisal and coverage must remain separate. Use its reasoning as an evidence-design caution, not as a prediction of this client’s outcome. [B-S12]

| Argument | Evidence needed | Strongest challenge | Proper limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local shingle repair cannot restore the affected area | Product identification; mapped damage; qualified, representative repairability assessment; manufacturer method; alternatives | Aging or unrepresentative testing drives the claim; repair is feasible | Do not manufacture damage or equate age with covered storm damage |
| Tile is unavailable or incompatible | Manufacturer/profile identification; dated sourcing and salvage checks; dimensions/interlock; method and quantity | Compatible stock or salvage exists | Discontinued is not identical to unavailable; appearance is not identical to functional incompatibility |
| Flat/low-slope assembly needs wider work | System/layer identification; mapped condition/moisture evidence with limits; causation; repair/recover alternatives | Maintenance or localized repair explains scope | No universal moisture threshold, destructive test or replacement rule supplied here |
| Matching undamaged surfaces | Actual policy/endorsement, controlled visual evidence, applicable HOA record if relevant | Matching exclusion or no coverage grant | Separately price disputed appearance-only work; do not hide it in physical repair |
| Code requires added work | Parcel/AHJ, adopted edition, local amendment, exact section, trigger and official interpretation if needed | Wrong jurisdiction, edition or repair trigger | Code compliance does not itself establish ordinance/law coverage |

Phoenix’s official page states that the 2024 Phoenix Building Construction Code was adopted June 18, 2025, effective August 1, 2025. That establishes a city adoption record only. A Phoenix-area mailing address does not identify whether Phoenix, another municipality, unincorporated Maricopa County or another authority controls. Verify permit/transition rules and roof-specific text before naming a requirement. No particular decking, underlayment, ventilation or whole-roof threshold has been verified for this unknown property. [B-S13]

Keep base repair and incremental ordinance/law costs separate, then examine the coverage grant, exclusion, sublimit and any incurred-cost condition. Manufacturer instructions, warranty preferences, HOA restrictions and adopted law have different authority. An instruction can support technical necessity without itself being an insurance promise. [B-S13, B-S16]

## 6. Claim-handling requirements: triggers before deadlines

R20-6-801(A) excludes workers’ compensation and title policies; the relevant general insurer provisions are distinct from its automobile-only subsection H. For a residential first-party claim, use the table below only after establishing the trigger and receipt date. The rule uses **working days** in some places and unqualified **days** in others. Track the latter as calendar-day periods, while having any actual legal due-date computation and holiday treatment checked. [B-S09]

| Requirement | Trigger and responsible party | Period and qualification | Locator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acknowledge claim notification | Insurer receives notice; notice to its agent counts | 10 working days, unless payment occurs within that period; nonwritten acknowledgment documented | E(1) |
| Respond to regulator inquiry | Insurer receives Department inquiry about claim | 15 working days | E(2) |
| Appropriate reply to pertinent communication | Claimant communication reasonably suggests response expected | 10 working days | E(3) |
| Forms/instructions/assistance | Insurer receives claim notification | Prompt; compliance within 10 working days satisfies E(1) | E(4) |
| Complete investigation | Claim notification | 30 days, unless it cannot reasonably be completed in that period | F |
| Accept/deny or explain need for more time | Insurer receives properly executed proof of loss | 15 working days; a policy-based denial references provision/condition/exclusion and is written | G(1)(a)–(b) |
| Continuing investigation explanation | Initial notice that more time is needed | 45 days from that notice and every 45 days thereafter while incomplete | G(1)(b) |
| Time-limit warning in continued negotiations | Direct negotiations with claimant who is neither attorney nor represented by attorney as rights may expire | Notice 30 days before first-party limit; 60 days for third-party claimant | G(4) |

G(1)(c) has a specific supported-arson-suspicion exception to G(1)(a)–(b), with its own reasonable-investigation standard. No facts invoke it here. Do not invent a general storm/catastrophe extension, and do not misstate the 30-day investigation standard as a universal 30-day payment deadline. A supplement is not necessarily a properly executed proof of loss. A contractor’s letter is not automatically a qualifying claimant communication under the rule’s definition. Record who sent it and on whose authority. [B-S09(B), (F)–(G)]

Section 20-461(A) addresses listed conduct with a frequency indicating a general business practice. Subsection D specifies an administrative remedy and disclaims a private right under that section. A disagreement does not by itself prove a violation or bad faith. The statute does not erase separate potential contract/common-law remedies; a qualified lawyer must evaluate those. Do not add threats or automatic damages to a reconciliation letter. [B-S08]

An optional requested reply date should say **requested scheduling date**, and it must not be labeled a statutory deadline without verified triggering facts. Neither this packet nor a complaint/appraisal request calculates or promises suspension of any contractual or legal deadline.

## 7. Keep gross scope and net insurance payment separate

The following is a data model, not a universal settlement formula:

| Field | Meaning and control |
|---|---|
| Gross contractor estimate | Price of the described construction scope, before insurance settlement adjustments |
| Proposed estimate revision | Sum of supported line deltas and separately recomputed taxes/fees/markups; may include clearly marked coverage-disputed work |
| Covered replacement-cost basis (RCV) | Valuation for eligible covered work under the actual policy; does not equal every contractor charge |
| Actual cash value (ACV) | Apply the contract’s valuation method and governing interpretation, not a universal software formula |
| Recoverable depreciation | Potential holdback subject to the policy’s completion, expenditure, notice and other conditions |
| Nonrecoverable depreciation | Amount not later payable under the applicable settlement terms; do not label as a holdback |
| Deductible | Apply correct amount/basis, occurrence and coverage allocation exactly once under the policy |
| Limits/sublimits | Separate base building and ordinance/law or other applicable caps; application order requires the policy |
| Prior payments | Date, coverage, gross/net basis, deductible already applied, depreciation releases, void/reissue status |
| Potential additional payment | Calculate only after verified coverage, valuation, conditions, limits, deductible and applicable prior payments |

Walker prohibited labor depreciation under the policy before the Arizona Supreme Court. The opinion analyzed replacement cost less depreciation, the omission of definitions, and the actual wording; it also explained that other policies can support different ACV methodologies. Compare the actual endorsement and distinguish future repair labor from labor embedded in pre-loss property value. Do not announce that labor is never depreciable under any Arizona policy. [B-S10 ¶¶ 4, 9 n.2, 23–29]

For a same-scope line comparison, compute `requested quantity × requested unit price − carrier quantity × carrier unit price`, with currency rounding and documented units. Recompute tax, overhead and profit only on verified eligible bases. An alternative repair and replacement scope must not both enter the same total. Never subtract a deductible or prior payment from each line; reconcile them separately at the correct policy level. This run supplies no live prices, universal markup, tax rate, waste percentage or loss amount. [Workflow calculation method; B-S16 supplies the policy-control premise.]
