# Write a precise and persuasive package

Build the factual argument before polishing sentences. Use one shared ledger for the memo, checklist, letter and appendix. Explain what should change, where, why, by how much, and what would defeat the request. A useful concession increases the precision of the remaining case.

For each issue: observation → disputed assumption → necessary work → evidence → calculation → policy/authority connection if applicable → strongest fair objection → reply or concession → exact requested action. Distinguish the carrier's supplied reason from an anticipated objection. A manufacturer's method is technical support, not a promise of coverage.

The memo should be formal enough to withstand close review and readable enough for the claim representative to act on. Use short issue titles and concrete nouns. Name the facet, component, estimate version, exhibit and calculation. Do not add a lawyer's tone by adding threats, Latin, unsupported conclusions or credentials. Describe actual review roles accurately.

The letter should normally fit on one page: request, main differences, correct amount and financial basis, attachments, and next action. A incomplete comparison can request missing evidence or reinspection without a complete dollar demand. Avoid implementation terms, audit jargon, stock gratitude and assertions that an estimate is wrong simply because it is lower.

## Weak and stronger examples

Weak: “Industry standards require full replacement and 10-and-10 overhead and profit. Please release all funds immediately.”

Stronger synthetic example: “Please review the installation quantity for facets A and B. Exhibit E-04 measures 25 squares of roof surface; line 12 allows 20. At the unchanged rate of $400 per square, the quantity correction is $2,000 before tax and markup. The takeoff already includes pitch. If your measurement uses a different surface boundary, please identify it so we can reconcile the two drawings.”

The stronger paragraph establishes a narrow question, shows arithmetic, addresses double slope and invites a specific contrary explanation. Its figures are fictional teaching values, not local prices.

## Editorial check

- Does the opening state a concrete requested action?
- Can the recipient find every material observation and amount in the cited source?
- Are facts, inferences, unresolved policy questions and requested work distinct?
- Do reductions, feasible repairs and defeating conditions remain visible?
- Is the letter's amount the same amount and basis used in the memo?
- Does each attachment exist and correspond to the same version?
- Is every named inspector/reviewer/sender a real participant, accurately described?
